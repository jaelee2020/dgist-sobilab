Research You Could Explore

Single microfibers are our starting point. We engineer flexible electrodes and soft sensors, then connect them with wearable interfaces, biomedical devices and robotic systems.

Single-Microfiber Soft Sensor Platforms

Flexible fiber electrodes and soft sensors that detect pressure, strain and contact, connecting materials engineering with wearable, biomedical and robotic systems.

Explore materials, electrodes and sensing devices

Wearable Sensors & Human–Robot Interfaces

Sensing fibers and textile electrodes integrated into gloves and clothing for movement monitoring, muscle signal recording and human–robot interaction.

Explore smart textiles and wearable interfaces

Electronic Sutures & Implantable Bioelectronics

Soft fiber devices combined with medical sutures for strain and pH sensing, alongside research on electrical stimulation and biodegradable electronics.

Explore fiber devices for biomedical research

Self-Sensing Actuators & Artificial Muscles

Soft actuators with integrated sensing to measure deformation and provide feedback for controlled movement in robotic and wearable systems.

Explore soft robotics and sensing feedback

Postdoctoral Research

Soft Sensors & Bioelectronics

We welcome inquiries from researchers with a Ph.D. in materials science, electrical or electronic engineering, mechanical engineering, biomedical engineering, or a related field. Tell us how your expertise could connect with a research question in soft sensing or bioelectronics.

Research Directions

  • Single-microfiber electrodes, conductive materials and soft mechanical sensors
  • Multimodal tactile devices and robotic contact perception toward Physical AI
  • Electronic textiles, wearable EMG sensing and human–robot interfaces
  • Electronic sutures, implantable sensing and biodegradable fiber electronics
  • Self-sensing soft actuators and artificial muscles

Potential Contributions

Depending on the research direction, a project could draw on your experience in:

  • Materials and fabrication process development
  • Device design, fabrication and performance evaluation
  • Experimental measurement and sensor signal analysis
  • Integration of devices with wearable, biomedical or robotic systems

Starting a Postdoctoral Inquiry

Please contact Prof. Jaehong Lee with:

  • A CV outlining your background and research experience
  • Links to representative publications
  • A short description of your research interests and their connection to SoBiLab

An inquiry can begin a discussion of research fit. Project scope, appointment availability and support arrangements are discussed individually.

Before You Reach Out

Do I need previous research experience?

Prior experience can be helpful. For an initial graduate or undergraduate conversation, tell us what you have studied and what you would like to learn. Your background and its connection to a possible project can be discussed.

Which backgrounds connect with this work?

Our research connects materials and fabrication with electronics, mechanical systems and biomedical applications. Explore the research overview to see where your interests might fit.

What should I include in my first email?

For a graduate or undergraduate inquiry, start with your current program and a few lines about your interests. A CV can provide additional context. For a postdoctoral inquiry, please include a CV, links to representative publications and a short description of your research interests.

Is a research inquiry an admission application?

No. A conversation with the lab helps explore research fit and possible next steps. Formal graduate applications follow DGIST's current admission requirements, schedule and evaluation process.

How can I ask about an undergraduate internship?

Include your university or program, research interests and possible participation period. The match with current research activities and the timing of a placement can then be discussed.

How can I learn about funding or appointment conditions?

Contact Prof. Lee to discuss your intended program or research direction. Funding and appointment arrangements depend on the role, project and relevant program requirements.
